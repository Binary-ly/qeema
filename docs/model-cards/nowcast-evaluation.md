## Caveat

Backtested on the synthetic six-month history, whose price process the generator defined — inflation, FX pass-through, a regional premium and decaying shocks. A model evaluated on data generated by known rules will do better than one facing real markets, so read these as an upper bound. The interval-coverage figure is the more transferable of the two: a model that cannot calibrate its own uncertainty on easy data will not manage it on hard data.

## Known weakness: the interval under-covers

Empirical coverage is **74.6%** against a nominal **80%**. The interval is
narrower than it claims, so roughly one true value in twenty falls outside a
band advertised as containing four in five.

This is not catastrophic — it is not the 55% that would make the interval
actively misleading — but it is the wrong direction to be wrong in. An interval
should over-cover rather than under-cover, because a reader who trusts an 80%
band is entitled to have it hold at least that often.

Two straightforward remedies, neither applied yet because both need
re-measurement rather than assertion:

1. Train the outer models at τ = 0.05 / 0.95 and publish the result as an 80%
   band, buying margin at the cost of some width.
2. Apply a conformal correction: measure the empirical quantile of residuals on
   a calibration split and widen by exactly that amount, which gives coverage
   guarantees that do not depend on the model being well specified.

Until one of those lands, the published interval should be read as approximately
75%, and this file says so rather than leaving the reader to assume 80%.
